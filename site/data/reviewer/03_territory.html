National territory

Syllabus IV.A: Art. I; Admin. Code, Book I, Sec. 3; RA 9522 (baselines, 2009); RA 12064 (Maritime Zones Act, 2024); RA 12065 (Archipelagic Sea Lanes Act, 2024); 4 cases and the 2016 Award.

The provision

Art. IThe national territory comprises the Philippine archipelago, with all the islands and waters embraced therein, and all other territories over which the Philippines has sovereignty or jurisdiction, consisting of its terrestrial, fluvial and aerial domains, including its territorial sea, the seabed, the subsoil, the insular shelves, and other submarine areas. The waters around, between, and connecting the islands of the archipelago, regardless of their breadth and dimensions, form part of the internal waters of the Philippines.

Two parts: (1) the archipelago with its islands and waters; (2) all other territories over which the Philippines has sovereignty or jurisdiction. The last sentence states the archipelagic doctrine: waters inside the archipelago are internal waters.

Territory versus maritime zones

The key distinction: sovereignty over territory is acquired or lost only by occupation, accretion, cession and prescription; maritime zones under UNCLOS give sovereign rights and jurisdiction measured from baselines. Baselines laws neither add nor lose territory (Magallona).

  • RA 9522 (baselines): treats the Kalayaan Island Group and Scarborough Shoal as a ‘regime of islands’ under UNCLOS Art. 121, outside the main baselines, without giving up any claim.
  • RA 12064 (Maritime Zones Act, 2024): defines the maritime zones in domestic law consistent with UNCLOS and the 2016 Award.
  • RA 12065 (Archipelagic Sea Lanes Act, 2024): designates sea lanes through which foreign ships and aircraft exercise archipelagic sea lanes passage.

Cases

  1. Magallona v. Ermita (2011): RA 9522 valid; UNCLOS is a treaty on sea-use rights, not on acquiring territory.
  2. South China Sea Arbitration (2016): China’s nine-dash-line historic rights invalid beyond UNCLOS; no Spratly feature generates an EEZ; China violated Philippine sovereign rights. The tribunal did not decide sovereignty over features.
  3. North Cotabato v. GRP (2008): the MOA-AD’s associative ‘Bangsamoro Juridical Entity’ and the promise to change the Constitution were unconstitutional.
  4. Cruz v. DENR (2000): IPRA survived a 7–7 vote; native title versus the Regalian Doctrine.
In a news question: West Philippine Sea incidents (water cannons, blocking resupply, reclamation): the question is usually about sovereign rights in the EEZ under UNCLOS and the Award, not ‘territory’. For a government agreement on joint exploration, add Art. XII, Sec. 2 (exploration of natural resources under full State control and supervision) and the treaty concurrence rule if it is an international agreement.
Exam trap: saying RA 9522 ‘gave up’ Kalayaan or Scarborough. It did not; it classified them as a regime of islands, which preserves the claim and is consistent with UNCLOS.

Last-minute checklist

  • Territory is acquired only by occupation, accretion, cession, prescription; baselines and UNCLOS do not change it.
  • Zones: 12, 24, 200 nautical miles; continental shelf.
  • Award (2016): historic rights extinguished to the extent incompatible with UNCLOS; Spratly features cannot generate an EEZ.
  • MOA-AD: the President cannot guarantee constitutional amendments.
