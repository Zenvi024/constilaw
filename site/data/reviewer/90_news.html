Political dynasties: the Court orders Congress to legislate

Kapatiran Party v. Congress (decision dated Aug. 26, 2026; released Sept. 16): Art. II, Sec. 26 is a mandatory command; 39 years of inaction is grave abuse; mandamus granted (by majority); Congress defines ‘political dynasty’; COMELEC cannot disqualify without the law. After the ruling, the Senate moved to seek an urgent certification for its bill (SB 1901, second-degree ban, pending on second reading); the House had passed HB 8389 on June 3.

  • Rules it triggers: self-executing provisions (Manila Prince; Art. II generally not enforceable); expanded judicial review of inaction; separation of powers (can the Court compel Congress?); and, for any dynasty law, whether a ban adds qualifications to constitutional offices (Sec. 26 is the constitutional source, unlike SJS v. DDB).
  • Practice: evaluate a senator’s critique; challenge to a dynasty law; in the fishbowl: the senator’s critique, out loud.

VP Sara Duterte’s second impeachment: from the House to the verdict

New complaints came once the one-year bar from Duterte v. House lapsed. The House Justice Committee found two sufficient in form, substance and grounds (March 2026), held probable-cause hearings without her and subpoenaed her SALNs (2007–2025), found probable cause unanimously on April 29, and the House impeached her on May 11, 2026, the first official impeached twice. The Senate received the Articles on May 14 and convened as an impeachment court on May 18; the trial began on July 6. The Court dismissed the Torreon and Duterte petitions against the House proceedings as moot on August 5 (Torreon).

  • The vote to convict: on September 23 the impeachment court ruled 13–1 that ‘all the Members of the Senate’ (Art. XI, Sec. 3(6)) counts only senators legally and actually able to take part, reversing the presiding officer’s July 6 ruling that 16 of 24 were needed. On September 30 the Supreme Court unanimously dismissed three petitions against that ruling on procedural grounds (standing, prematurity).
  • One article left: on October 13 the court let the prosecutors withdraw three of the four Articles, leaving Article 2 (unexplained wealth, SALN non-disclosure, business interests). The defense opened on November 9; on November 13 the court’s spokesperson said the verdict was expected 20 to 25 days after the defense rests.
  • Rules it triggers: the one-year bar and initiation (Francisco, Gutierrez, Duterte); mootness (Torreon); ‘forthwith’ (Generillo); the Senate’s rules and its presiding officer; verba legis (‘Members present’ against ‘all the Members’) and Avelino; the judgment and its effects (Re: Corona; Sereno).
  • Practice: the two-thirds count; fishbowl cards: the second impeachment, the moot petitions, ‘forthwith’, the presiding officer, one article left. The whole story is on the other-powers map.

The Senate leadership fight: Cayetano, Gatchalian and Avelino

On May 11, 2026, the Senate elected Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano Senate President, 13–9 with two abstentions. After Sen. Jinggoy Estrada’s arrest on plunder charges (June 1) and Sen. dela Rosa’s return to hiding, Cayetano’s bloc boycotted sessions. On June 3, twelve senators, citing Avelino v. Cuenco, declared a quorum of the 22 deemed available, declared all posts vacant and elected Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian President Pro Tempore; they conceded they lacked the 13 votes to elect a Senate President. Cayetano’s bloc went to the Supreme Court on June 16. On June 17, in a special session called by the President, 13 senators elected Gatchalian Senate President, and Cayetano conceded. The Blue Ribbon flood-control inquiry resumed under a new chair on June 8.

  • Rules it triggers: a majority of all the Members to elect officers (Sec. 16(1)) against a majority for a quorum (Sec. 16(2)); Avelino on coercive jurisdiction; Santiago v. Guingona on internal matters; special sessions (Sec. 15); inquiries and contempt (Sec. 21; Balag; Ong 2025).
  • Practice: fishbowl: was the June 3 session valid?; the flood-control inquiry.

Sen. dela Rosa, the ICC warrant and ‘protective custody’

Absent since November 2025, Sen. Ronald dela Rosa appeared on May 11, 2026 (the day the ICC unsealed its warrant for the crime against humanity of murder) and voted to elect Sen. Cayetano Senate President. When NBI agents tried to arrest him in the Senate, the chamber placed him under ‘protective custody’; he left on May 14. Earlier, on April 22–23, the ICC Appeals Chamber upheld its jurisdiction over former President Duterte despite the withdrawal from the Rome Statute, and Pre-Trial Chamber I confirmed all three counts and committed him to trial.

  • Rules it triggers: privilege from arrest (Sec. 11: not more than six years, while in session); Jalosjos and Trillanes; the Senate’s powers under Sec. 16, none of which shields a member from law enforcement; withdrawal from treaties (Pangilinan) and surrender under RA 9851, Sec. 17 (Bayan Muna).
  • Practice: immunity and the Senate’s actions; fishbowl: the ICC and the surrender.

The Vice President’s immunity claim

Charged with three counts of grave threats in Quezon City (bail posted September 5, 2026), the Vice President asked the Supreme Court on September 10 to act on her petition for certiorari and a TRO, claiming ‘limited procedural immunity’ as an impeachable officer. The trial court deferred her arraignment on September 11 and again on October 9. The petition was pending at the last reports.

  • Rules it triggers: only the President is immune during the term (Soliven; De Lima v. Duterte; Estrada); In re Gonzalez (impeach first) applies to officers who must be lawyers and to charges that would remove them.
  • Practice: fishbowl: can she be prosecuted now?

A new Chief Justice, and the Carandang ruling

The JBC opened the search on August 29, 2026; on October 29 it sent five nominees for Chief Justice and eleven for Justice Lazaro-Javier’s seat (vacant November 16). The President appointed Justice Rodil Zalameda the 28th Chief Justice on November 5, a day before Chief Justice Gesmundo’s retirement at 70; Zalameda took his oath on November 6 and serves until 2033. Separately, in a decision released May 2, 2026, the Court affirmed that the Office of the President had no disciplinary authority over Overall Deputy Ombudsman Carandang (Gonzales III, stare decisis).

  • Rules it triggers: the JBC list and the 90 days (Art. VIII, Secs. 4(1) and 9; De Castro; Aguinaldo); when an appointment is complete (Velicaria-Garafil); the Ombudsman’s independence and the limits of the removal power.
  • Practice: fishbowl: the Chief Justice appointed a day early; the deputy ombudsman.

The budget: Lagman v. Sotto and the unprogrammed funds

In Lagman v. Sotto (G.R. No. 271059; decision dated October 7, 2026, released October 20), the Court struck down Congress’s ₱449.5-billion increase in 2024 unprogrammed appropriations, the ₱17.99-billion increase in the 2025 DPWH Special Road Fund and a ₱12.75-billion increase in special accounts, as violations of Art. VI, Sec. 25(1); it held that unprogrammed appropriations are valid but must stay within the constitutional ceiling and have clear limits, voided 2026 GAA provisions releasing them on revenue conditions with too few safeguards, and applied the operative fact doctrine to completed 2024 and 2025 programs. Earlier, in Pimentel v. House (Dec. 3, 2025), the Court voided the 2024 GAA provision that swept ₱60 billion of PhilHealth funds to the Treasury. The President’s 2027 budget (₱7.2 trillion) cuts unprogrammed appropriations to about ₱112 billion.

  • Rules it triggers: Sec. 25(1) (no congressional increase), 25(2) (riders), 25(5) (augmentation by the President only, from savings), 29(3) (special funds); Belgica 2013 and 2019; Araullo (excess-revenue certification; operative fact); the bicameral committee only reconciles.
  • Practice: unprogrammed appropriations; a 2027 fund sweep; fishbowl: Lagman v. Sotto.

Barangay and SK elections moved to 2028 (RA 12326)

Signed September 24, 2026: the November 2, 2026 BSKE moves to November 2028, with five-year terms; Sec. 5 speaks of the ‘extension of their terms’. Atty. Macalintal challenged it (one-subject rule, legislative appointment, ‘endless extensions’). Reported on November 6, 2026: the Court upheld the law in a decision dated October 14, 2026, penned by Justice (now Chief Justice) Zalameda, saying the right to vote does not guarantee a particular election frequency or term length so long as elections stay regular, fixed and predictable. The decision is not in the files; read it before relying on the details.

  • Rules it triggers: the postponement guidelines in Macalintal v. COMELEC (2023); hold-over versus term extension; the title rule (Giron).
  • Practice: resolve the petition.

BARMM: the first parliamentary elections

RA 12317 (signed March 25, 2026) moved the first BARMM parliamentary elections to September 14, 2026, after the Court voided the districting law (BAA No. 77; reconsideration denied in a resolution promulgated April 8 and released July 31). The elections were held on September 14 after four postponements; the COMELEC reported an 84.34% turnout.

  • Rules it triggers: Congress and the organic act (Art. X; Kida); postponement (Macalintal 2023); the COMELEC’s powers (Art. IX-C, Sec. 2; Sula); the President’s general supervision (Art. X, Sec. 16).
  • Practice: fishbowl: who decides the BARMM elections.

Treaties and visiting forces

The Senate concurred in the ASEAN Extradition Treaty on September 14, 2026 (Resolution No. 69; all 20 senators present voted yes), and in the Philippines–France Status of Visiting Forces Agreement on October 8, 2026 (17–0).

  • Rules it triggers: two-thirds of all the Members of the Senate (Art. VII, Sec. 21: 16 of 24); foreign troops only under a treaty (Art. XVIII, Sec. 25; Saguisag; Nicolas).
  • Practice: fishbowl: two concurrences.

Minors and social media

In October 2026 the House and the Senate approved separate bills restricting minors’ access to social media: the Senate version bars everyone under 18 and requires age verification; the House version bars accounts under 13 and allows 13- to 17-year-olds with parental consent. The versions still had to be reconciled.

  • Rules it triggers: a bill is not ripe for review (Montesclaros); the bicameral committee; police power (lawful subject, lawful means; White Light); Art. II, Secs. 12–13.
  • Practice: fishbowl: the social media ban.

Last-minute checklist

  • Kapatiran: duty vs content vs enforcement.
  • Impeachment: initiated = filed + referred; one year; ‘forthwith’ = reasonable time; ‘all the Members’ (the September 23 ruling, and the text against it); removal and disqualification only.
  • Senate officers: 13 of 24; quorum: Avelino’s coercive jurisdiction.
  • Arrest privilege: six years and in session; the Senate cannot grant custody. The ICC keeps jurisdiction over acts before March 17, 2019.
  • Immunity: the President only. In re Gonzalez: lawyers, removal.
  • Chief Justice: JBC list, 90 days, De Castro (early list), Garafil (vacancy and acceptance).
  • Budget: no increase (unprogrammed included), no riders, no transfers, special funds stay special.
  • Election postponements: Macalintal 2023 guidelines.
