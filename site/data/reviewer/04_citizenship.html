Citizenship

Syllabus IV.B.1: Art. IV; Admin. Code, Book I, Secs. 5–9; RA 9139; RA 8171; RA 9225. 17 cases. Tested in 2015 (Poe; citizenship by statute) and 2016 (dual citizens in government).

The provisions

Art. IV, Secs. 1 to 5Section 1. The following are citizens of the Philippines: (1) Those who are citizens of the Philippines at the time of the adoption of this Constitution; (2) Those whose fathers or mothers are citizens of the Philippines; (3) Those born before January 17, 1973, of Filipino mothers, who elect Philippine citizenship upon reaching the age of majority; and (4) Those who are naturalized in accordance with law.
Section 2. Natural-born citizens are those who are citizens of the Philippines from birth without having to perform any act to acquire or perfect their Philippine citizenship. Those who elect Philippine citizenship in accordance with paragraph (3), Section 1 hereof shall be deemed natural-born citizens.
Section 3. Philippine citizenship may be lost or reacquired in the manner provided by law.
Section 4. Citizens of the Philippines who marry aliens shall retain their citizenship, unless by their act or omission, they are deemed, under the law, to have renounced it.
Section 5. Dual allegiance of citizens is inimical to the national interest and shall be dealt with by law.

Natural-born or naturalized

The Philippines follows jus sanguinis. A natural-born citizen needs no act to acquire or perfect citizenship. Everyone else is naturalized, except those who elect under Sec. 1(3), who are deemed natural-born.

  • Tecson (2004): an illegitimate child of a Filipino father is Filipino if filiation is proved.
  • David v. SET (2016): foundlings are natural-born.
  • Co v. HRET (1991): election may be implied from acts (voting, holding office) for those already treated as Filipino.
  • Republic v. Sagun (2012): formal election under CA 625 needs a sworn statement, oath, and registration within a reasonable time (about 3 years).
  • Bengson v. Cruz (2001): repatriation restores natural-born status.

Naturalization

  • Judicial (CA 473): strict compliance; privilege, not a right. Go v. Republic (credible witnesses; petition contents), Go Pei Hung (certificate of arrival), Mahtani (lucrative occupation).
  • Administrative (RA 9139, 2000): for aliens born and residing in the Philippines since birth, through a Special Committee on Naturalization.
  • Legislative: Congress may naturalize a person by a law (a private bill, originating in the House under Art. VI, Sec. 24). The person is naturalized, not natural-born.
  • Derivative: minor children of a naturalized father (Tan Co).
Exam trap (2015 midterm, RA 10685 for Peter Wallace): a person naturalized by statute cannot be a Senator or Supreme Court Justice (natural-born required). For a local office, the LGC requires only citizenship plus residence and the other qualifications, so the strongest answer is that he may run for local office if he has those qualifications. Loss and reacquisition

  • Lost by naturalization abroad, express renunciation, oath to another country, and other grounds under CA 63. Conduct can be express renunciation (Willie Yu).
  • Reacquired by naturalization, repatriation (RA 8171; PD 725; RA 2630) or under RA 9225. Repatriation is filed with the Special Committee on Naturalization, not the RTC (Angat). It retroacts to the application (Frivaldo).

Dual citizenship and RA 9225

Dual citizenship (arising from the concurrent laws of two States, often by birth) is not dual allegiance (a positive act of loyalty to another State). Art. IV, Sec. 5 leaves dual allegiance to Congress (Calilung).

RA 9225 for candidates (Sobejana-Condon; Chua; Maquiling)
  1. Take the oath of allegiance (to reacquire or retain citizenship).
  2. For elective office: make a personal and sworn renunciation of all foreign citizenship before an officer authorized to administer oaths, at the latest when filing the COC.
  3. Keep acting consistently: using a foreign passport after renouncing recants the renunciation (Maquiling).
For appointive office, RA 9225 requires the appointee to renounce foreign citizenship before an authorized officer before assuming office (and to meet the office’s other qualifications).

These steps apply only to those who reacquired citizenship after naturalization abroad. A dual citizen by birth need not renounce; filing the COC is itself a renunciation (Gana-Carait, 2022).

Update (2025): when a winner is disqualified, Maquiling and Chua proclaimed the next-ranked qualified candidate. Mangudadatu v. COMELEC (2025) abandoned that rule; the vacancy is now filled by succession (e.g., the vice-mayor or vice-governor).
In a news question (2016 midterm, CSC barring dual citizens): an agency cannot bar dual citizens from government service by memorandum; Art. IV, Sec. 5 leaves dual allegiance to Congress, and RA 9225 already provides the conditions (renunciation for public office). The CSC lacks authority to add a disqualification (Calilung).

Last-minute checklist

  • Four classes of citizens; natural-born definition; Jan. 17, 1973 election rule.
  • Offices requiring natural-born status; local office needs only citizenship.
  • Three modes of naturalization; strict compliance.
  • RA 9225: oath, then sworn personal renunciation (candidates); passport use recants; dual by birth exempt (Gana-Carait).
  • Repatriation restores natural-born status (Bengson); retroacts (Frivaldo); filed with the Special Committee (Angat).
  • Second-placer rule abandoned (Mangudadatu, 2025).
