Congress: salaries, privileges and disqualifications

Syllabus V.A.4: Art. VI, Secs. 10 to 14; RA 10530; 11 cases. Tested in 2024 (Liban and RA 10530) and 2013 (arrest of a congressman).

The provisions

Art. VI, Secs. 10, 11, 13, 14Section 10. The salaries of Senators and Members of the House of Representatives shall be determined by law. No increase in said compensation shall take effect until after the expiration of the full term of all the Members of the Senate and the House of Representatives approving such increase.
Section 11. A Senator or Member of the House of Representatives shall, in all offenses punishable by not more than six years imprisonment, be privileged from arrest while the Congress is in session. No Member shall be questioned nor be held liable in any other place for any speech or debate in the Congress or in any committee thereof.
Section 13. No Senator or Member of the House of Representatives may hold any other office or employment in the Government, or any subdivision, agency, or instrumentality thereof, including government-owned or controlled corporations or their subsidiaries, during his term without forfeiting his seat. Neither shall he be appointed to any office which may have been created or the emoluments thereof increased during the term for which he was elected.
Section 14. No Senator or Member of the House of Representatives may personally appear as counsel before any court of justice or before the Electoral Tribunals, or quasi-judicial and other administrative bodies. Neither shall he, directly or indirectly, be interested financially in any contract with, or in any franchise or special privilege granted by the Government, or any subdivision, agency, or instrumentality thereof, including any government-owned or controlled corporation, or its subsidiary, during his term of office. He shall not intervene in any matter before any office of the Government for his pecuniary benefit or where he may be called upon to act on account of his office.

Salaries

An increase takes effect only after the full term of all members of both Houses who approved it has expired (PHILCONSA v. Mathay). It cannot be achieved indirectly through retirement benefits (Ligot v. Mathay).

  • ‘All the Members’ of both chambers who approved the raise: in PHILCONSA the RA 4134 increase could not take effect before December 30, 1969, when the last senators who voted for it finished their terms. The delay deters legislators from voting themselves raises.
  • A retirement gratuity is compensation; computing it on a raise the member could not receive is an indirect increase (Ligot).

Freedom from arrest

Privilege applies only if
  1. the offense is punishable by not more than six years’ imprisonment; and
  2. Congress is in session (regular or special, not recess).
It does not cover offenses punishable by more than six years, and it never exempts a convicted or lawfully detained member (Jalosjos; Trillanes v. Pimentel).
  • History (Jalosjos): 1935, all offenses except treason, felony and breach of the peace, during attendance at sessions; 1973, offenses punishable by not more than six years, during attendance, with the Batasan bound to surrender the member within 24 hours after adjournment; 1987, offenses punishable by not more than six years, while Congress is in session (attendance not required).
  • Election does not free a detained member: voters knew of his confinement; temporary leave is only for emergencies; detention prisoners, like convicts, cannot hold office while detained (People v. Maceda), and high office is not a substantial distinction for equal protection (Jalosjos; Trillanes).
2013 midterm (Dimaporo, malversation punishable by more than 6 years, arrested in hospital): the arrest is legal; the privilege covers only offenses punishable by not more than six years. A detained member may be counted in computing the House’s membership but cannot insist on attending sessions (Jalosjos).

Speech and debate

The privilege covers utterances and acts in the performance of legislative functions: floor speeches, votes, bills, committee proceedings (Jimenez v. Cabangbang). It does not cover press releases, open letters or media interviews (Jimenez; Trillanes v. Castillo-Marigomen). It protects against being questioned ‘in any other place’, not against the member’s own chamber (Osmeña) or against the chamber’s discipline (Pobre).

  • Protected acts (Jimenez): (1) floor speeches while in session; (2) statements made or votes cast in the halls of Congress while in session; (3) bills introduced, whether in session or not; (4) other official acts, inside or outside Congress, in the discharge of legislative or committee duties.
  • The privilege attaches because the statement furthers legislation, not because a lawmaker made it; press statements are ‘political in nature, rather than legislative’ (Trillanes v. Castillo-Marigomen). Its purpose is to let members speak with the fullest liberty, free from the resentment of the powerful (Osmeña).

Incompatible and forbidden offices

Incompatible office (first sentence)Forbidden office (second sentence)
Which officesAny other office or employment in the government, its subdivisions, agencies, instrumentalities, GOCCs and subsidiariesAny office created, or whose emoluments were increased, during the term for which he was elected
EffectHe may accept, but forfeits his seat (Adaza)He cannot be appointed at all during that term, even if he resigns
  • Adaza applied the 1973 rule (Art. VIII, Sec. 10, as amended), whose only exceptions were Prime Minister and Cabinet member; the 1987 Constitution has no Cabinet exception. Taking the legislative oath vacated the governorship.
  • Do not confuse Art. IX-B, Sec. 7 (no elective official is eligible for appointment to any public office during his tenure; resigning after the appointment does not cure it, Flores v. Drilon) with Art. VI, Sec. 13, under which a legislator may accept another office but forfeits his seat.

Liban v. Gordon: the PNRC chairmanship is not a government office (the PNRC is sui generis, an auxiliary of government, not a GOCC), so a senator who chairs it keeps his seat. The 2009 ruling that RA 95 was unconstitutional (Congress cannot create a private corporation by special charter, Art. XII, Sec. 16) was reversed in 2011 because it was not the lis mota and the PNRC is not an ordinary private corporation.

  • The test in Liban: the PNRC Chairman is elected by a private-sector-controlled Board and is appointed by neither the President nor a department head, so he is not a government official or employee.
2024 Q4: how RA 10530 answers Liban. Liban raised: (1) is the PNRC a GOCC (so a legislator chairing it forfeits his seat)? (2) can Congress create the PNRC by special charter when Art. XII, Sec. 16 bars special charters for private corporations? RA 10530 (2013) settles both by statute: it recognizes the PNRC as an independent, autonomous, non-governmental organization auxiliary to the authorities of the Government in the humanitarian field, consistent with the Geneva Conventions. It is neither a GOCC nor a private corporation formed for profit, so (1) its chairmanship is not a government office under Sec. 13, and (2) its charter is not barred by Art. XII, Sec. 16. Mention that the 2011 Resolution had already called the PNRC sui generis; RA 10530 codifies it.

Sec. 14: no personal appearance as counsel before courts, tribunals or administrative bodies; this cannot be evaded by buying shares to intervene as a ‘stockholder’ (Puyat v. De Guzman). A detained senator-petitioner’s request to argue her own case was denied (Pangilinan v. Cayetano, Notice, 2018).

  • In Puyat, a stockholder intervening for his own interest is ordinarily not appearing as counsel; the token ten shares, bought after his appearance as counsel was objected to, showed an indirect appearance.
  • De Lima asked to appear as a party, not as counsel, so Sec. 14 was not the ground; the denial rested on her detention, and her co-petitioners could argue the case.

Sec. 12 (disclosure of financial interests and conflicts of interest) and Sec. 20 (COA audit of Congress’s books, with an annual itemized list of amounts paid to each Member) complete the accountability provisions.

Last-minute checklist

  • Salary increase: after the full term of all members of both Houses who approved it.
  • Arrest privilege: offenses up to 6 years, while in session; not for detention after charge for graver crimes or conviction.
  • Speech: legislative acts only; not media; not immunity from one’s own chamber.
  • Incompatible (forfeits seat) versus forbidden (cannot be appointed) offices.
  • Liban 2009 and 2011; RA 10530: PNRC is independent, autonomous, non-governmental, auxiliary to government.
