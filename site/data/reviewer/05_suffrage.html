Suffrage

Syllabus IV.B.2: Art. V; RA 9189 as amended by RA 10590 (overseas voting); 7 cases. Tested in the 2015 finals (No Bio, No Boto; registration deadline).

The provisions

Art. V, Secs. 1 and 2Section 1. Suffrage may be exercised by all citizens of the Philippines, not otherwise disqualified by law, who are at least eighteen years of age, and who shall have resided in the Philippines for at least one year and in the place wherein they propose to vote, for at least six months immediately preceding the election. No literacy, property, or other substantive requirement shall be imposed on the exercise of suffrage.
Section 2. The Congress shall provide a system for securing the secrecy and sanctity of the ballot as well as a system for absentee voting by qualified Filipinos abroad. The Congress shall also design a procedure for the disabled and the illiterates to vote without the assistance of other persons. Until then, they shall be allowed to vote under existing laws and such rules as the Commission on Elections may promulgate to protect the secrecy of the ballot.

Qualifications versus regulation

Qualifications are fixed by the Constitution: citizenship, age 18, one year in the Philippines and six months in the place of voting. Congress may disqualify (“not otherwise disqualified by law”) but cannot impose a literacy, property, or other substantive requirement. Registration is not a qualification; it is the procedure by which qualifications are determined, and may be reasonably regulated.

Is the new condition valid?
  1. Is it a qualification or a substantive requirement (e.g., passing a test, paying a fee, owning property)? If so, invalid.
  2. Is it part of registration or procedure? Valid if reasonable. Where it burdens the fundamental right, apply strict scrutiny: compelling interest and least restrictive means (Kabataan).
  3. Was it made by the right body? COMELEC must stay within the law (Palatino).
  • Kabataan v. COMELEC (2015): biometrics validation is registration, not a qualification; passes strict scrutiny.
  • AKBAYAN v. COMELEC (2001): no special registration within the 120-day prohibitive period.
  • Palatino v. COMELEC (2009): COMELEC cannot close registration earlier than the law allows unless it shows the pre-election acts cannot reasonably be done; deadline voided.
  • Ceniza v. COMELEC (1980): HUC voters may be excluded from provincial elections; reasonable classification.
  • Macalintal v. COMELEC (2003): overseas absentee voting is an exception to the residence requirement; the affidavit of intent to return is valid.

Residence

Residence in election law means domicile. A new domicile requires bodily presence, intent to remain, and intent to abandon the old (Gallego v. Verra). Absence for work or study, or forced exile, does not lose domicile (Romualdez v. RTC). The same rule governs candidates (Aquino; Marcos; Mangudadatu).

In a news question: COMELEC deactivations, registration deadlines, voting fees, ID requirements, or proposals to bar some group from voting. Ask: qualification or regulation? Which body acted, and within what law? Is it inside the 120-day (regular) or 90-day (special) prohibitive period?

COMELEC sets the registration deadline for the May 2028 elections on September 30, 2027, citing the new automated system, and suspends registration for a week during the filing of COCs. Valid?

Answer: Not without proof. RA 8189, Sec. 8 allows continuing registration until 120 days before a regular election. COMELEC’s stand-by power to fix other dates applies only if the pre-election acts cannot reasonably be done within that period, which it must show (Palatino; AKBAYAN). Administrative convenience during COC filing is not enough. The deadline and suspension should be set aside unless COMELEC proves necessity.

Last-minute checklist

  • Qualifications: citizen, 18, 1 year in the Philippines, 6 months in the place.
  • No literacy, property, or other substantive requirement; registration is procedure.
  • 120 days (regular) / 90 days (special) no-registration period; Palatino versus AKBAYAN.
  • Domicile: presence, animus manendi, animus non revertendi.
  • Overseas voting is an exception to residence (Macalintal 2003); a JCOC approving COMELEC rules is invalid.
