Powers of Congress, its limits, delegation, and titles of bills

Syllabus V.A.7 and V.A.8(a)(1): plenary power; substantive limits (P&S 31–37); procedural limits (ABAKADA); titles of bills; plus delegation and the publication of IRRs, which past midterms test heavily (2023 oversight committee; 2024 Bulacan IRR; 2020 finals on IRRs). 9 cases.

Plenary legislative power

Art. VI, Sec. 1The legislative power shall be vested in the Congress of the Philippines which shall consist of a Senate and a House of Representatives, except to the extent reserved to the people by the provision on initiative and referendum.

The grant is broad and plenary: Congress may legislate on any matter of common interest unless the Constitution forbids it or lodges the power elsewhere (Torralba: creating municipalities). Only Congress may create legislative districts, and so provinces and cities (Sema). Congress also has plenary power over election schedules, subject to suffrage and due process (Macalintal, 2023).

Substantive limits (Gatmaytan, P&S)

  1. Unconstitutional laws. Congress cannot pass laws beyond its power, that infringe constitutional principles, or whose purpose or effect violates the Constitution. Courts presume constitutionality and strike down only on a clear breach.
  2. Supermajority votes. Congress cannot require a higher vote to amend or repeal a law; each House passes bills by majority with a quorum. One Congress cannot bind the next.
  3. Irrepealable laws. No law can be made beyond repeal; this would deprive later legislatures of their power.
  4. Retroactive laws. Laws are generally prospective; procedural laws may apply to pending cases; ex post facto laws and bills of attainder are prohibited (Art. III, Sec. 22).
  5. The Supreme Court’s autonomy. Congress cannot exempt anyone from court fees (fiscal autonomy) or alter rules of procedure (Art. VIII, Sec. 5(5)); it cannot increase the Court’s appellate jurisdiction without its advice and concurrence (Art. VI, Sec. 30).
  6. Other express limits: the Bill of Rights; no adding qualifications to constitutional offices (SJS v. DDB); no titles of nobility (Sec. 31); no interpreting the Constitution by statute (Endencia); the money provisions (Secs. 24 to 29).
Gatmaytan: his book lists these five substantive limitations (unconstitutional laws, supermajority votes, irrepealable laws, laws with retroactive effect, fiscal autonomy of the Court). When a question involves a law that ‘cannot be amended except by three-fourths’ or ‘shall not be repealed’, cite this list.

Procedural limits: the legislative veto (ABAKADA)

After enactment, Congress may only scrutinize (budget hearings, question hour, confirmation) and investigate (inquiries in aid of legislation). It may not supervise. Any law that lets Congress, a committee, or individual members approve, disapprove, or take part in the implementation of a law violates separation of powers, and also bicameralism and presentment, because it lets Congress act with the force of law without passing a bill (ABAKADA). The same reasoning killed the pork barrel (Belgica) and the JCOC over COMELEC’s rules (Macalintal, 2003).

Is the oversight committee valid?
  1. Does it only review, monitor, require reports, hold hearings and recommend legislation? Valid (scrutiny and investigation).
  2. Does it approve rules, budgets of an agency, or projects; deputize or direct executive agencies; or otherwise take part in enforcement? Invalid (legislative veto or execution by Congress).
  3. Save the rest of the law if separable (ABAKADA kept RA 9335 except Sec. 12).
2023 Q3 (Tax Code Sec. 290-A, Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on Illicit Trade): reviewing BIR and BOC performance, requiring reports, holding hearings and recommending remedial legislation are valid oversight (scrutiny and investigation in aid of legislation). But the power to deputize the BIR, BOC, PNP and NBI ‘in undertaking its duties’ lets Congress direct law enforcement: execution belongs to the President (Art. VII, Sec. 17). That clause is void under ABAKADA; the rest may stand. Also: ‘in aid of legislation’ cannot cure an executive role.

Delegation of legislative power

Potestas delegata non delegari potest. Congress cannot delegate the power to make the law (to decide what the law shall be), but may confer discretion in its execution.

Valid delegation (both tests)
  1. Completeness: the law sets forth the policy to be executed, leaving nothing for the delegate to decide but how to carry it out.
  2. Sufficient standard: the law fixes limits that are sufficiently determinate or determinable, to which the delegate must conform (e.g., ‘public interest’, ‘reasonable’, ‘just and equitable’ have been accepted when read with the law’s policy).
Permitted delegations: (1) tariff powers to the President (Art. VI, Sec. 28(2)); (2) emergency powers to the President (Sec. 23(2)); (3) to the people (initiative and referendum); (4) to local governments; (5) to administrative agencies (subordinate legislation).
  • Undue delegation: People v. Vera (provincial boards deciding whether the Probation Act applies). Subdelegation by an agency to private parties is invalid (KMU: operators setting their own fares).
  • Valid: Agustin v. Edu; Zabal (proclamation as subordinate legislation under disaster laws); Garcia v. Executive Secretary (tariffs).
Valid IRR (Gatmaytan’s book, citing Lokin)
  1. Its promulgation is authorized by the legislature;
  2. it is within the scope of the authority given;
  3. it is promulgated in accordance with the prescribed procedure; and
  4. it is reasonable (germane to the law and consistent with its standards).
An IRR cannot amend, expand or contradict the law it implements.

Publication and effectivity

Laws take effect 15 days after publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation, unless the law provides otherwise (Civil Code, Art. 2 as amended by EO 200). ‘Unless otherwise provided’ refers to the date, not to dispensing with publication: all statutes, including those of local application, and all rules that implement laws must be published (Tañada v. Tuvera, 1985 and 1986). Interpretative rules, purely internal rules, and letters of instruction to subordinates need not be published. Under the Administrative Code (Book VII), agencies must also file rules with the University of the Philippines Law Center (ONAR); rules take effect 15 days after filing unless a different date is fixed.

2024 Q3 (Bulacan Freeport Act, Sec. 29: DTI to issue IRR within 90 days, in consultation with DOF, NEDA, the Province and a representative of the airport project; effective 15 days after publication in a newspaper of general circulation): (1) No legislative veto: Congress has no role after enactment, unlike ABAKADA. (2) Consultation with a private project representative is not a delegation of rule-making to a private party; the DTI alone promulgates, but it must not let the private developer dictate (KMU). (3) The 90-day period is directory; a late IRR is not void. (4) Publication in one newspaper of general circulation satisfies Tañada and EO 200, but the IRR must also be filed with ONAR under the Administrative Code to bind the public. (5) The law itself must supply the policy and standards (completeness and sufficient standard). Conclusion: Sec. 29 is valid.

Title and one subject (Art. VI, Sec. 26(1))

Art. VI, Sec. 26(1)Every bill passed by the Congress shall embrace only one subject which shall be expressed in the title thereof.

Two purposes (Lidasan): to prevent hodgepodge or log-rolling legislation, and to inform legislators and the public of the bill’s subject so there is no surprise or fraud. Construed liberally: the title need not catalogue every provision; it is enough that it is comprehensive enough to include the general object, and that all provisions are germane to it (Giron). A misleading title voids the law, wholly if the parts are inseparable (Lidasan). A title also limits how far an enabling law can be read (Cruz v. Paras).

2015 midterm (anti-dynasty rule in the SK Reform Act): the SK law’s title concerns the SK’s structure and eligibility; a rule on SK officials’ relatives is germane to eligibility for SK office, so it is not a rider and does not violate the one-subject rule. Since SK office is statutory, adding qualifications is valid (the ‘adding qualifications’ bar applies to offices whose qualifications the Constitution fixes). The key marked ‘valid’.

Last-minute checklist

  • Plenary power, except as limited; only Congress creates districts (Sema).
  • Five substantive limits from P&S; plus no added qualifications; no interpreting the Constitution.
  • Post-enactment: scrutiny and investigation only; no approval or execution role (ABAKADA).
  • Delegation: completeness plus sufficient standard; five permitted delegations; no subdelegation.
  • IRR: authorized, within scope, proper procedure, reasonable; published; filed with ONAR.
  • Title rule: no hodgepodge, no surprise; liberal construction; germaneness.
