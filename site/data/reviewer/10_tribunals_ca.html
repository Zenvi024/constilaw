Electoral tribunals and the Commission on Appointments

Syllabus V.A.5–6: Art. VI, Secs. 17 to 19; 12 cases. Tested in 2013 (Muhlach v. Fuentebella: COMELEC or HRET?) and 2015 (Poe: forum).

The provisions

Art. VI, Secs. 17 and 18Section 17. The Senate and the House of Representatives shall each have an Electoral Tribunal which shall be the sole judge of all contests relating to the election, returns, and qualifications of their respective Members. Each Electoral Tribunal shall be composed of nine Members, three of whom shall be Justices of the Supreme Court to be designated by the Chief Justice, and the remaining six shall be Members of the Senate or the House of Representatives, as the case may be, who shall be chosen on the basis of proportional representation from the political parties and the parties or organizations registered under the party-list system represented therein. The senior Justice in the Electoral Tribunal shall be its Chairman.
Section 18. There shall be a Commission on Appointments consisting of the President of the Senate, as ex officio Chairman, twelve Senators, and twelve Members of the House of Representatives, elected by each House on the basis of proportional representation from the political parties and parties or organizations registered under the party-list system represented therein. The chairman of the Commission shall not vote, except in case of a tie. The Commission shall act on all appointments submitted to it within thirty session days of the Congress from their submission. The Commission shall rule by a majority vote of all the Members.

When does the tribunal take over from COMELEC?

HRET or SET jurisdiction begins when three things concur (Reyes v. COMELEC, 2013)
  1. A valid proclamation;
  2. a proper oath (for the House, before the Speaker in open session);
  3. assumption of office (on June 30).
Before that, COMELEC keeps jurisdiction over the winner’s qualifications. If COMELEC’s ruling on the proclamation itself is still pending, the tribunal does not acquire jurisdiction (Codilla). If the COC was finally cancelled before the election, there is no ‘member’ for the tribunal to judge (Velasco).
2013 midterm (Muhlach v. Fuentebella): Fuentebella had been proclaimed; Muhlach’s remedy is an election protest in the HRET once Fuentebella becomes a member (proclamation, oath, assumption). Before assumption, Reyes v. COMELEC says COMELEC still has jurisdiction, so read the dates. The circulated key chose the HRET.

Nature of the tribunals

  • ‘Sole judge’: independent of the chamber and of the courts; non-partisan (‘a non-political body in a sea of politicians’, Gatmaytan’s book).
  • Members have security of tenure; a chamber cannot remove a member for voting against his party (Bondoc v. Pineda). Valid causes: end of term, death or disability, resignation from the party he represents, formal affiliation with another party, removal for other valid cause.
  • All members must sit; the Justices alone cannot decide (Abbas). A rule requiring at least one Justice for a quorum is valid (Reyes v. HRET).
  • The Supreme Court reviews tribunal decisions only for grave abuse (e.g., annulling precincts without substantial evidence: Abayon).
  • Each chamber chooses its members of the tribunal and the CA; claimants go to the chamber first (Pimentel v. HRET).

Commission on Appointments

Proportional representation in the CA
  1. Senate seats per party = (party’s senators ÷ 24) × 12; House seats computed the same way on the House membership.
  2. Fractions cannot be rounded up at another party’s expense (Guingona v. Gonzales); the CA can function with fewer than 12 from a chamber.
  3. A party must be big enough for at least one seat; being ‘minority’ is not enough (Coseteng).
  4. Membership may be changed when political alignments change permanently (Daza v. Singson), not for temporary alliances.
  5. Challengers must have standing and first raise the matter in the chamber (Drilon v. De Venecia).

The CA confirms the appointments listed in Art. VII, Sec. 16 (department heads; ambassadors, public ministers and consuls; officers of the armed forces from colonel or naval captain; other officers whose appointments are vested in the President by the Constitution). Congress cannot add offices requiring confirmation by statute (a structural point that also appears in the finals coverage).

Last-minute checklist

  • Tribunal: 3 Justices + 6 members; senior Justice chairs; ‘sole judge’ of election, returns and qualifications.
  • Jurisdiction: proclamation + oath + assumption (Reyes v. COMELEC).
  • Bondoc: no removal for party disloyalty. Abbas: all must sit. Abayon: grave abuse review.
  • CA: Senate President + 12 + 12; chair votes only to break ties; acts within 30 session days; majority of all Members.
  • Guingona: no rounding up fractions; Daza: permanent realignments; Coseteng: minority status alone gives no seat.
