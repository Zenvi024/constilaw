Congress: officers, quorum, rules, discipline and the Journal

Syllabus V.A.3: Art. VI, Secs. 15 and 16; 16 cases. Tested in 2023 (Teves expulsion), 2013 (counting a hospitalized member for quorum), 2015 finals (absentee members and the Ombudsman).

The provisions

Art. VI, Sec. 16(1) The Senate shall elect its President and the House of Representatives, its Speaker, by a majority vote of all its respective Members. Each House shall choose such other officers as it may deem necessary.
(2) A majority of each House shall constitute a quorum to do business, but a smaller number may adjourn from day to day and may compel the attendance of absent Members in such manner, and under such penalties, as such House may provide.
(3) Each House may determine the rules of its proceedings, punish its Members for disorderly behavior, and, with the concurrence of two-thirds of all its Members, suspend or expel a Member. A penalty of suspension, when imposed, shall not exceed sixty days.
(4) Each House shall keep a Journal of its proceedings, and from time to time publish the same, excepting such parts as may, in its judgment, affect national security; and the yeas and nays on any question shall, at the request of one-fifth of the Members present, be entered in the Journal. Each House shall also keep a Record of its proceedings.
(5) Neither House during the sessions of the Congress shall, without the consent of the other, adjourn for more than three days, nor to any other place than that in which the two Houses shall be sitting.

Officers and internal rules

  • Only the Senate President and Speaker are constitutionally elected (majority of all Members). Everything else (minority leader, committee chairs) is for the chamber (Santiago v. Guingona; Baguilat).
  • Courts do not enforce purely internal rules (Arroyo v. De Venecia), except when (a) a constitutional requirement is involved (quorum, voting, publication: Pimentel v. Senate Committee of the Whole), or (b) third parties’ rights are affected (Pacete).

Quorum

A majority of the members within the coercive jurisdiction of the chamber (Avelino v. Cuenco: 12 of 23 senators present in the country). Quorum is presumed to continue unless challenged through the chamber’s procedure; the Journal and the enrolled bill control (Tinio v. Duterte, 2023).

2013 midterm (Dimaporo in hospital detention): he remains a Member and is counted in the base for computing the majority of the House; whether he is ‘present’ for quorum is a different question. Members cannot be compelled to attend while lawfully detained (Jalosjos). Read the question’s exact verb (‘counted as a member’ versus ‘counted as present’).
In a news question (2015 finals: Ombudsman asked to compel absent members to attend): Sec. 16(2) gives each House the power to compel attendance of absent members ‘in such manner, and under such penalties, as such House may provide’. The remedy is internal. The Ombudsman cannot intrude into a constitutional function of Congress; separation of powers. Members’ salaries are fixed by law, so absence alone does not create a disallowance unless a rule provides a penalty.

Discipline of members

Valid suspension or expulsion
  1. Ground: disorderly behavior, as the House determines (wide deference: Osmeña v. Pendatun).
  2. Vote: two-thirds of all Members (not just those present).
  3. Suspension: not more than 60 days. Expulsion has no cap but is the gravest penalty.
  4. Due process: notice and a chance to be heard (Pimentel v. Senate Committee of the Whole).
  5. No conviction required; it is distinct from criminal liability.
  • Alejandrino (1924): courts would not order reinstatement of a suspended senator (decided before the 60-day cap).
  • Osmeña (1960): the speech privilege does not shield a member from his own House; taking up other business does not waive discipline.
  • De Venecia v. Sandiganbayan (2002): a court’s preventive suspension under RA 3019 is not discipline and is valid.
  • Pobre v. Defensor-Santiago (2009): discipline for floor speech belongs to the chamber, not the courts.
In a news question (2023 Q1, Teves): no conviction is needed and the Court defers on what is disorderly. But you can argue for him: (1) the House already suspended him twice for 60 days for the same absence, so expelling him for it evades the cap and punishes twice; (2) grounds like a terrorist designation by the Executive and a personal video are not disorderly behavior as a Member; (3) due process. Know both sides.

Sessions: regular session from the fourth Monday of July until 30 days before the next opening (Sec. 15); the President may call special sessions; joint sessions for war, canvassing presidential votes, martial law revocation or extension, and the SONA.

Last-minute checklist

  • Speaker and Senate President: majority of all Members; other officers per chamber rules.
  • Quorum: majority within coercive jurisdiction (Avelino); presumed to continue (Tinio).
  • Discipline: disorderly behavior; two-thirds of all Members; suspension max 60 days; no conviction needed.
  • Internal rules not enforced by courts, except constitutional requirements or third-party rights.
  • Enrolled bill conclusive; Astorga exception; Journal for constitutionally required entries.
