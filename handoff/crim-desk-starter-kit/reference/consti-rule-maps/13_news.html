Political dynasties: the Court orders Congress to legislate

Kapatiran Party v. Congress (decision dated Aug. 26, 2026; released Sept. 16): Art. II, Sec. 26 is a mandatory command; 39 years of inaction is grave abuse; mandamus granted (by majority); Congress defines ‘political dynasty’; COMELEC cannot disqualify without the law. After the ruling, the Senate moved to seek an urgent certification for its bill (SB 1901, second-degree ban, pending on second reading); the House had passed HB 8389 on June 3.

  • Rules it triggers: self-executing provisions (Manila Prince; Art. II generally not enforceable); expanded judicial review of inaction; separation of powers (can the Court compel Congress?); and, for any dynasty law, whether a ban adds qualifications to constitutional offices (Sec. 26 is the constitutional source, unlike SJS v. DDB).
  • Practice: evaluate a senator’s critique; challenge to a dynasty law.

VP Sara Duterte’s impeachment: who counts as ‘all the Members’?

The House impeached the Vice President a second time on May 11, 2026 (257 votes). On September 23, the Senate impeachment court ruled 13–1 that ‘all the Members of the Senate’ (Art. XI, Sec. 3(6)) counts only senators legally and actually able to take part, excluding those detained, in hiding or incapacitated. On September 30, the Supreme Court dismissed three citizens’ petitions on procedural grounds (standing, prematurity). The trial pauses after October 15 for the 2027 budget.

  • Rules it triggers: verba legis and the deliberate contrast between ‘Members present’ and ‘all the Members’; quorum and coercive jurisdiction (Avelino); a detained member is still a member (Jalosjos); ripeness and standing; Francisco’s limits on the political question doctrine.
  • Practice: the two-thirds count.
Coverage: impeachment (Art. XI) is not in your midterm syllabus. Use the story for interpretation, judicial review and quorum, not for impeachment procedure.

Sen. dela Rosa, the ICC warrant and ‘protective custody’

Absent since November 2025, Sen. Ronald dela Rosa appeared on May 11, 2026 (the day the ICC unsealed its warrant for the crime against humanity of murder) and voted to elect Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano as Senate President, 13–9–2. When NBI agents tried to arrest him in the Senate, the chamber placed him under ‘protective custody’; he left on May 14. Sen. Sherwin Gatchalian has been Senate President since June 17, 2026.

  • Rules it triggers: privilege from arrest (Sec. 11: not more than six years, while in session); Jalosjos and Trillanes; the Senate’s powers under Sec. 16 (officers by majority of all Members, quorum, compelling attendance, discipline), none of which includes shielding a member from law enforcement.
  • Practice: immunity and the Senate’s actions.

Barangay and SK elections moved to 2028 (RA 12326)

Signed September 24, 2026: the November 2, 2026 BSKE moves to November 2028, with five-year terms; Sec. 5 speaks of the ‘extension of their terms’. Atty. Macalintal’s petition (one-subject rule, legislative appointment, ‘endless extensions’) is pending; the OSG says the term of barangay officials is a political question.

  • Rules it triggers: the postponement guidelines in Macalintal v. COMELEC (2023): postponement is the exception; it needs important, substantial or compelling reasons; election fatigue and divisiveness are not enough; intervals must not be unduly long; it must be narrowly tailored. Also hold-over (tenure) versus term extension, and the title rule (Giron).
  • Practice: resolve the petition.

The budget: unprogrammed funds and the PhilHealth ruling

Reps. Erice and De Lima challenge the ₱150.9-billion unprogrammed appropriations in the 2026 GAA; the Court heard oral arguments in April with petitions on the 2024 and 2025 budgets and asked for memoranda in July. The President’s 2027 budget (₱7.2 trillion, submitted in August) cuts unprogrammed appropriations to about ₱112 billion. In Pimentel v. House (Dec. 3, 2025), the Court voided the 2024 GAA provision that swept ₱60 billion of PhilHealth funds to the Treasury.

  • Rules it triggers: Sec. 25(1) (no congressional increase), 25(2) (riders), 25(5) (augmentation by the President only, from savings), 29(3) (special funds); Belgica 2019 (standby funds valid with standards); Araullo (excess-revenue certification); the GAA cannot amend substantive law.
  • Practice: unprogrammed appropriations; a 2027 fund sweep.
